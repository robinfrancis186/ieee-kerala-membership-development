01What is IEEE

The world’s largest technical professional organization.

IEEE is dedicated to advancing technology for the benefit of humanity. Its members are engineers, scientists, and allied professionals whose interests are rooted in electrical and computer sciences, engineering, and related disciplines.

Mission

The core purpose of IEEE is to foster technological innovation and excellence for the benefit of humanity.

Vision

IEEE will be essential to the global technical community and to technical professionals everywhere, and be universally recognized for the contributions of technology and of technical professionals in improving global conditions.

03How IEEE is organised

From IEEE to your Student Branch.

IEEE is organised two ways at once: geographically, from Regions down to your branch, and technically, through Societies and Councils that have chapters everywhere.

  1. GlobalIEEE

    Board of Directors and global operations.

  2. 10 RegionsRegion 10 · Asia and Pacific

    Regions 1 to 6 cover the United States; 7 is Canada, 8 Europe, Middle East and Africa, 9 Latin America, and 10 Asia and Pacific.

  3. CouncilIEEE India Council

    Coordinates the Sections in India.

  4. SectionIEEE Kerala Section

    Your local home. Every member is assigned to a Section by address.

  5. Local unitsSubsections, Society chapters, affinity groups, Student Branches

    Malabar and Kochi Subsections; WIE, YP, Life Members, and SIGHT.

  6. Inside a branchStudent Branch Chapters and branch affinity groups

    A Society chapter or WIE group run by students in the same college.

04Membership and benefits

What membership gives you.

IEEE exists because of its members, and members excel because of IEEE. Membership opens technical content, people, funding, and leadership roles well beyond the classroom.

ieee.org/join

Membership grades

  1. Student MemberUndergraduate carrying at least 50% of a normal full-time programme.
  2. Graduate Student MemberPostgraduate students, on the same 50% rule.
  3. MemberProfessionals with evidence of competence in an IEEE-designated field.
  4. Senior MemberRecognises significant professional experience and achievement.
  5. FellowIEEE’s highest grade, by nomination, for extraordinary accomplishment.
  6. Life MemberLong-standing members who meet age and service criteria.

Five things to do after joining

  1. Complete your profile: education, employment, technical interests, and address at ieee.org/profile.
  2. Find a Society community that matches your interests.
  3. Sign in to IEEE Collabratec and connect with local volunteers.
  4. Download your membership certificate from Collabratec.
  5. Browse member benefits and discounts at ieee.org/benefits.

06Dues and rebate

There is no fixed dues waterfall.

IEEE dues are not divided into a guaranteed sequence of amounts for your Student Branch, Section, Council, and Region. The rules use a shared allocation pool and separate eligibility-based rebates.

2025 MGA Operations Manual
  1. 01Core IEEE dues

    IEEE Bylaws distinguish core IEEE dues from optional, member-choice dues for technical Societies. Society add-ons are not included in this estimate.

  2. 0212% MGA allocation

    The current MGA manual allocates 12% of member dues to Regions, Sections, Chapters, Affinity Groups, and Student Branches as a whole.

  3. 03Formula and eligibility

    Region allocations use prior-year membership, number of Sections, and relative travel cost. Other geographic units use their own rebate schedule and reporting rules.

  4. 04Local unit receives and distributes

    Subunit rebates, including Student Branch rebates, are disbursed through the Section's bank account. Sections and Geographic Councils manage or transfer subunit funds under MGA rules.

The 12% is an aggregate allocation, not the share for any one member's Branch or Section. A Student Branch rebate is a separate branch-level calculation, not a percentage of the member's individual payment. See IEEE Bylaw I-108 and the MGA allocation rules.

10Build your branch

A stronger branch is built one habit at a time.

Build a repeatable member experience, not a calendar full of disconnected events. Each activity should help someone feel welcome, learn something, contribute, or take the next step.

  1. Welcome

    Make joining and attending a first event feel clear. Introduce a real person, a small role, and a next date.

    01

  2. Create value

    Co-design technical, career, and community activities around what members say they need.

    02

  3. Grow leaders

    Give members ownership in small, supported steps. Let new volunteers lead a piece before leading a whole event.

    03

  4. Hand over well

    Keep a shared activity log, budget notes, templates, and introductions so knowledge survives an election.

    04

Activity ideas

  • Expert talks and workshops by industry practitioners
  • Technical competitions and hackathons
  • Industry visits
  • Peer tutorials for junior students
  • Branch projects that serve the community
  • Participation in IEEE conferences and Section events
  • A branch newsletter and a maintained website
  • Science and technology awareness programmes
  • Personality development sessions
  • Quiz competitions

Plan the next 90 days

Choose a small number of useful actions. Put an owner and a date beside each one, then review what changed for members.

Days 01–15

Listen and map

Ask current and prospective members what would make the branch worth their time. Review your member journey and current reporting status.

  • Pick one audience to reach
  • Assign one owner per action
Days 16–30

Make a clear invitation

Run a short introduction or membership clinic with a specific next step and a real follow-up date.

  • Explain benefits without overpromising
  • Invite people into a small role
Days 31–60

Deliver member value

Offer a hands-on technical, professional, or humanitarian activity with room for members to contribute.

  • Use a feedback question
  • Report the activity in vTools
Days 61–90

Keep the loop going

Share outcomes, recognize contributors, ask who wants to lead next, and record what the next committee needs.

  • Follow up with first-time attendees
  • Set the next 90-day review

03
